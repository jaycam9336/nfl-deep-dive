The founding — Canton, 1920

On September 17, 1920, representatives met in Canton, Ohio and formed the American Professional Football Association (APFA). Eleven franchises were represented (including the Canton Bulldogs, Decatur Staleys, Chicago Cardinals, Akron Pros, Cleveland Indians, Dayton Triangles, Hammond Pros, Muncie Flyers, Rock Island Independents, Rochester Jeffersons; Massillon sent word it would not join). Jim Thorpe, the Canton Bulldogs star, was unanimously elected first president — his fame lent the new league credibility. The meeting minutes set a $100 membership fee (accounts say it was never actually paid). Sources: Pro Football Hall of Fame + NFL.com (2-source).

Only two clubs from that founding meeting still operate today: the Cardinals (now Arizona Cardinals — the franchise traces to 1898, the oldest continuously operating pro football organization) and the Decatur Staleys, who became the Chicago Bears. Source: Pro Football Hall of Fame.

In 1922 the APFA renamed itself the National Football League. Sources: Pro Football Hall of Fame “Football Firsts” + NFL.com (2-source).

Timeline

1920

APFA founded in Canton, Sep. 17; Jim Thorpe first president; $100 membership fee (never paid per accounts). HOF + NFL.com

1922

APFA renamed National Football League. HOF + NFL.com

1925

New York Giants join for a $500 franchise fee. HOF expansion-fees table (single authoritative source)

1933

Philadelphia Eagles and Pittsburgh Pirates (now Steelers) join for $2,500 each. HOF expansion-fees table

1960

Dallas Cowboys join NFL for $1 million expansion fee; the rival AFL launches the same year with original franchises at $25,000 each. HOF expansion-fees table

1961–1968

Expansion wave: Minnesota Vikings $1M (1961), Atlanta Falcons $8.5M (1966), Miami Dolphins $7.5M AFL (1966), New Orleans Saints $8.5M (1967), Cincinnati Bengals $7.5–$8M AFL (1968). HOF expansion-fees table

1966

AFL–NFL merger announced June 8, 1966 after secret Hunt/Schramm meetings: separate schedules through 1969, common draft from 1967, a championship game from Jan. 1967, full merger in 1970. Pete Rozelle commissioner. HOF 1966 page + Britannica (2-source)

1967

First AFL–NFL World Championship Game (retroactively Super Bowl I), Jan. 15, 1967, Los Angeles: Green Bay 35, Kansas City 10. “Super Bowl” name first used in 1969. Britannica + HOF (2-source)

1970

Merger completed: AFL becomes the AFC, old NFL the NFC, in one NFL. Britannica + HOF (2-source)

1976

Seattle Seahawks and Tampa Bay Buccaneers join for $16 million expansion fees each — the modern 28-team league takes shape. HOF expansion-fees table

1995

Carolina Panthers and Jacksonville Jaguars join for $140 million expansion fees each. HOF table + Panthers official history for Carolina (2-source for CAR)

2002

Houston Texans join for a $700 million expansion fee — the 32nd and most recent expansion team. The league has been at 32 teams since. HOF expansion-fees table (single authoritative source)

Relocation era (examples)

Relocation, not expansion, reshaped the map in recent decades — e.g. the Raiders: Oakland (1960) → Los Angeles (1982) → Oakland (1995) → Las Vegas (2020). Per-team moves are detailed, with sources, in each team's Origins & Ownership tab. NFL Operations team history + NFL.com (2-source for the Raiders example)

Expansion fees are not sale prices

An expansion fee is what a new owner paid the league to create a new franchise. A sale price is what a buyer paid a previous owner for an existing team. A valuation (e.g. Forbes' yearly estimate) is neither — no money changed hands. This app labels all three separately and never presents a valuation as a price.
